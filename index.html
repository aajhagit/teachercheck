English Grammar Checker for Students: Fix Mistakes Without Losing Your Voice

✎ Teacher's note for students:

Your professors want to read your perspective, not a synthetic chatbot's prose. Most automated writing tools completely rewrite your sentences with corporate jargon, often triggering AI detection algorithms. TeacherCheck marks only genuine grammatical mistakes directly above your words, leaving your ideas, structure, and natural voice completely untouched.

The Problem with Blanket AI Paraphrasers in School

When writing essays, research summaries, and assignments, students frequently turn to AI rewriters to polish their drafts. However, heavy-handed rewriters introduce two significant problems for academic writing:

  • Loss of Personal Academic Voice: Generative rewriters flatten personal nuance and replace concise phrasing with formulaic, inflated vocabulary (e.g., changing "This study shows" into "It is of paramount significance to observe that this empirical inquiry elucidates").
  • Unintended AI-Detection Flags: When a paraphraser rewrites whole paragraphs, the resulting burstiness and perplexity scores match generative AI models, putting your authentic coursework under unwarranted scrutiny.
  • Passive Dependence vs. Active Learning: Simply accepting an automated full-sentence rewrite prevents you from understanding why an error occurred, meaning the same mistake is repeated on exams and timed essays.

4 Common Sentence Errors in Student Papers (and How to Fix Them)

In student submissions, the vast majority of points lost to mechanics stem from four recurring sentence-level errors. Here is how a teacher approaches each one:

1. Run-On Sentences and Comma Splices

A comma splice happens when two independent clauses (complete thoughts that could stand alone as separate sentences) are joined with only a comma.

Draft Sentence Teacher's Correction Why It Matters
The experiment failed, we did not calibrate the sensors in time. The experiment failed because we did not calibrate the sensors in time. (Or: The experiment failed; we did not calibrate...) A comma alone cannot join two independent clauses without a coordinating conjunction.

2. Subject-Verb Disagreement with Intervening Phrases

When prepositional phrases or parenthetical clauses sit between the subject and verb, writers often mistakenly match the verb to the nearest noun.

Draft Sentence Teacher's Correction Why It Matters
The list of required laboratory readings are available on the portal. The list of required laboratory readings is available on the portal. The singular subject is list, not the plural noun readings inside the prepositional phrase.

3. Ambiguous Pronoun Reference

Pronouns like this, that, it, or they must point clearly to a single preceding noun. When two possible referents exist, readers must guess your meaning.

Draft Sentence Teacher's Correction Why It Matters
The professor handed the student the syllabus, and then he left the room. After handing the syllabus to the student, the professor left the room. Clarifies who he refers to, eliminating confusion between the professor and the student.

4. Tricky Homophone Confusions

Academic essays are frequently marked down for quick typographical errors involving words that sound identical. Check our in-depth guides for the two most common student traps:

  • It's vs. Its: Never confuse the contraction (it is) with the possessive pronoun (belonging to it).
  • Affect vs. Effect: Master the difference between the action verb and the resulting noun.

IELTS Preparation Guide Task 1 & Task 2 Focus

IELTS Writing Grammar Checker: Targeted Feedback for Band 7+ Accuracy

✎ IELTS Examiner insight:

Grammatical Range and Accuracy accounts for 25% of your IELTS Writing score. To achieve Band 7 or higher, the official public descriptors require that you produce "frequent error-free sentences" and show good control of grammar and punctuation. AI paraphrasers often introduce unnatural, unidiomatic phrasing that lowers your Lexical Resource score. TeacherCheck pinpoints exact grammatical errors while keeping your arguments natural.

Understanding the Band 6 vs Band 7 Grammar Threshold

The difference between Band 6 and Band 7 in IELTS Writing (both Academic and General Training) is rarely about having "fancier" vocabulary. Rather, it comes down to error density and sentence control:

  • Band 6 Descriptor: "Uses a mix of simple and complex sentence forms. Makes some errors in grammar and punctuation, but they rarely reduce communication."
  • Band 7 Descriptor: "Uses a variety of complex structures. Produces frequent error-free sentences. Has good control of grammar and punctuation but may make a few characteristically non-systematic errors."

The key hurdle for most test-takers stuck at Band 6.0 or 6.5 is not vocabulary—it is the presence of recurring, systematic slips in articles, prepositions, and clause combinations.

4 High-Frequency Errors That Lower IELTS Writing Scores

1. Missing or Misplaced Definite Articles with Abstract Nouns

Candidates often over-apply the definite article (the) before general uncountable concepts, or omit it before specific post-modified nouns.

Candidate Draft Examiner Correction Grammar Rule
The society should invest more in education. Society should invest more in education. When speaking of human society in general, do not use the. Only use the when specifying a particular society (e.g., the society of ancient Rome).
Due to rapid development of technology... Due to the rapid development of technology... When a noun is followed by an of phrase, the definite article is required.

2. Faulty Parallelism in Complex Task 2 Arguments

When listing causes, effects, or solutions, all items in the series must share the same grammatical form (e.g., all gerunds, all infinitives, or all noun phrases).

Candidate Draft Examiner Correction Grammar Rule
Governments should focus on building hospitals, improving transit, and to reduce pollution. Governments should focus on building hospitals, improving transit, and reducing pollution. All three items coordinated by and must use parallel gerund forms (-ing).

3. Conditionals with Mixed Tenses

Complex structures like second and third conditionals demonstrate grammatical range, but tense mismatches frequently spoil the execution.

Candidate Draft Examiner Correction Grammar Rule
If governments had implemented these policies earlier, the situation will be different today. If governments had implemented these policies earlier, the situation would be different today. In mixed conditionals combining past condition with present result, use would + bare infinitive, not will.

4. Misusing Homophones in Timed Conditions

Examiners notice when stress leads to careless spelling errors. Review our rules for it's vs its and affect vs effect to protect your punctuation and lexical scores.

How to Practice IELTS Writing with TeacherCheck

During test prep, do not paste entire essays into generic AI tools that rewrite them wholesale. Instead, adopt a deliberate practice routine:

  • Sentence-by-Sentence Analysis: Check your topic sentences and complex argument sentences individually in TeacherCheck.

Grammar Rule Guide Punctuation & Mechanics

It's vs. Its: The 5-Second Rule to Never Confuse Them Again

✎ The 5-second teacher rule:

Whenever you write it's, expand it in your head to "it is" or "it has". If the sentence still makes sense, keep the apostrophe. If it sounds broken or nonsense, use its without an apostrophe.

Quick Comparison at a Glance

Word Grammatical Role Meaning Example
It's (with apostrophe) Contraction Short for "it is" or "it has" It's a beautiful morning. (It is)
It's been raining all day. (It has)
Its (no apostrophe) Possessive Determiner Belonging to it (shows ownership) The dog wagged its tail.
The company released its report.
Its' (apostrophe at end) Never correct Does not exist in the English language Never use this form under any circumstance.

Why Is This So Confusing?

The confusion is natural because in English, we usually add 's to nouns to show possession (e.g., Sarah's book, the cat's toy, the university's policy).

However, personal pronouns never take apostrophes for possession:

  • He → His (not he's)
  • She → Hers (not her's)
  • They → Theirs (not their's)
  • Who → Whose (not who's)
  • It → Its (not it's)

Just as you would never write "the book is her's", you should never write "the dog wagged it's tail".

6 Real Examples Tested with the 5-Second Rule

Example 1: "The software lost (it's / its) database connection."

Test: "The software lost it is database connection." → Makes no sense.
Correct: The software lost its database connection.

Example 2: "(It's / Its) time to submit your essay."

Test: "It is time to submit your essay." → Makes complete sense.
Correct: It's time to submit your essay.

Example 3: "Every tree loses (it's / its) leaves in autumn."

Test: "Every tree loses it is leaves in autumn." → Broken English.
Correct: Every tree loses its leaves in autumn.

Example 4: "(It's / Its) been three months since the release."

Test: "It has been three months since the release." → Contraction of it has.
Correct: It's been three months since the release.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is "its'" ever a valid English word?

No. Some people assume that if a plural possessive word like students' has an apostrophe after the s, then its' must exist. It does not. Its' is 100% incorrect in all contexts.

Can "it's" stand for anything other than "it is"?

Yes, it's can also contract it has when followed by a past participle: "It's been a pleasure" = "It has been a pleasure".

Grammar Rule Guide Word Choice & Vocabulary

Affect vs. Effect: How to Choose the Right Word Every Single Time

✎ The RAVEN mnemonic:

Remember the acronym RAVEN: Remember Affect = Verb, Effect = Noun. In over 95% of everyday, academic, and professional writing, affect is the action you take, and effect is the result you see.

The Basic Distinction

Word Part of Speech Meaning Quick Test
Affect Verb (Action) To influence, to produce a change in, to impact Can you substitute "influence" or "change"? If yes, use affect.
Effect Noun (Thing) A result, an outcome, a consequence Can you put "the", "an", or "side" in front of it? If yes, use effect.

Detailed Breakdown with Examples

When to Use Affect (Verb)

Use affect when something is acting upon something else:

  • The rainy weather will affect our weekend soccer tournament. (The weather influences the tournament).
  • High inflation rates directly affect household purchasing power.
  • Lack of sleep significantly affects your memory retention.

When to Use Effect (Noun)

Use effect when referring to the outcome or result:

  • The new policy had a noticeable effect on team morale. (A result you can see).
  • Does this allergy medicine cause any drowsy side effects?
  • Scientists are actively measuring the greenhouse effect.

The Two Advanced Exceptions (for Confident Writers)

While RAVEN holds for almost all everyday situations, English has two rare exceptions worth knowing:

  • Effect as a Verb (To Bring About): Used in formal contexts meaning to cause or execute a change. Example: "The committee hopes to effect lasting systemic change." (Meaning: to bring that change into existence).
  • Affect as a Noun (Emotional Display): Used in clinical psychology to describe an observed emotional demeanor. Example: "The patient exhibited a flat affect during the interview."

Quick Diagnostic Quiz: Test Yourself

1. "The tax reform will (affect / effect) small business owners."

Answer: affect. It acts as a verb (will influence).

2. "What was the long-term (affect / effect) of the decision?"

Answer: effect. It is preceded by the and functions as a noun (consequence).

3. "Music has a profound (affect / effect) on my study focus."

Answer: effect. Preceded by a profound; it is a noun meaning outcome or influence.
